How they're made

The laser does the shape.
I do everything after.

A cover takes about forty minutes of hands-on work after it comes off the bed. That's the whole reason these look the way they do — and the reason the first batch is only fifty.

  1. 1

    Cut

    The freeform outline and the switch opening are cut in one pass from a single board. Every plate starts life as a curve, not a rectangle.

  2. 2

    De-soot

    Laser cutting leaves a smoky halo along every edge. It comes off by hand, one cover at a time, until the wood underneath is clean.

  3. 3

    Sand

    Three grits, finishing fine enough that the edge reads as soft in your hand before you ever look at it.

  4. 4

    Seal

    A hand-rubbed finish that pulls the grain forward and stands up to a decade of fingertips hitting the same spot.

  5. 5

    Cap

    Screw caps go in last, matched to the color you picked — so the hardware either disappears completely or pops on purpose.

Meet the maker

Hi, I'm Kathryn.

I spent a long time redoing a 1962 house — new paint, good lamps, the whole thing — and then kept catching the same beige plastic rectangle out of the corner of my eye. It's the piece of your house you touch more than any other, and somehow it's the one piece nobody designs.

So I started cutting my own. The blob shape came from the laminate patterns and coffee tables I'd been staring at for two years, and the blue screw caps came from a hunch that hardware should be part of the design instead of something you try to hide.

I design them, cut them, sand them and seal them myself, in batches small enough that I can still look at every one before it ships. That's the whole company. It's me.

— Kathryn
