How they're made

The laser cuts the shape.
I do everything else.

A cover takes about forty minutes of hands-on work after it comes off the bed. That's the whole reason these look the way they do, and the reason the first batch is only fifty.

  1. 1

    Cut

    The freeform outline and the switch opening are cut in one pass from a single board.

  2. 2

    Clean

    Laser cutting leaves a smoky halo along every edge. It comes off by hand, one cover at a time, until the wood underneath is clean.

  3. 3

    Sand

    Three grits, finishing fine enough that the edge reads as soft in your hand before you ever look at it.

  4. 4

    Seal

    A hand-rubbed finish that pulls the grain forward and stands up to a decade of fingertips hitting the same spot.

  5. 5

    Cap

    Screw caps go in last, matched to the color you picked — so the hardware either disappears completely or pops on purpose.

Meet the maker

Hi, I'm Kathryn.

I spent a long time making my house feel and look like me, colorful and considered. But one thing always bothered me, the plain white plastic rectangle light switches in every single room. It's a piece of the house I touch more than any other, and somehow it's the one piece nobody designs.

So I started designing my own. The blob shape came from years of sketching and drawing inspiration from organic forms and undulating shapes. I stumbled across the screw caps on another product and knew it was meant to be. It makes the hardware part of the design instead of something that tries to blend in.

After living with them for a year, I knew I needed to share them. So I'm cutting them, sanding them, and sealing them myself, in batches small enough that I can still look at every one before it ships. That's the whole company! It's me.

Help me make these a reality by signing up for the first batch, only 50, and you can help pick what cap colors I stock. I hope your home feels more you soon!

— Kathryn
